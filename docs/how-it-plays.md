# How it plays

The fastest way to understand the game: what the player actually does, minute by minute. Read this before the systems documents.

## The verbs
| Verb | What the player does | What the world does back |
|---|---|---|
| **Move** | Run, slide and dash across floating islands with momentum | Smooth when calm; wind, dust and cracking edges when frantic |
| **Stop** | Stand still, on purpose | Sound clears, the Cinder locks, hidden paths and ignition seams appear |
| **Align** | Face the Cinder's heading or a lantern, then hold | Suit seams glow; paths stabilise |
| **Ignite** | Hold still at an ignition column | One of the six sky lines lights |
| **Strike** | Fire arrows from the Sacred Arc, one per opening | Enemies shatter on an open frame; split if you spam |
| **Parry** | Time a guard against a Burn Knight | Its shield drops |
| **Ride** | Commit to heat currents and the Fire Dragon's flight | Clean flight if calm; turbulence if forcing |

There is no inventory, no skill tree, no shop, no HUD. The only "meter" is the world.

## A minute of play: Stage 3, the arena
1. Elias walks out onto a circular obsidian plateau. Five vents pulse. The Fire Eye watches overhead.
2. Two glass figures appear. The player fires, fires, fires. Each hit splits an enemy in two. Wind rises, dust lifts, the sound turns harsh. The player is now in **Entropy**.
3. A platform edge cracks. A storm builds. Mashing makes it worse.
4. The player runs behind a tall monolith, breaks line of sight and, a little confused, stops. Two seconds pass. The noise fades, the storm falls away and the Cinder brightens.
5. The player now waits for a glass figure to open its guard and fires **once**. It shatters. The player has just discovered the game's rule without any text.
6. A heavy Burn Knight arrives. Hitting its shield does nothing. A well-timed parry drops it. One arrow ends it.

That discovery, "when I stop, things get easier", is the game. Every stage is the same idea in a new skin.

## The three loops
| Loop | Length | What repeats |
|---|---|---|
| **Moment** | Seconds | Rush, feel the world fight back, stop, feel it calm |
| **Stage** | 9-17 minutes | Explore, align, hold still at the column, survive a trial, a line ignites, the world brightens |
| **Game** | About 85 minutes | Six lines, rising toward a Throne visible from the first island, then the ring of fire |

## What a player sees
No health bar, no minimap, no objective text. They see the world: six bars in the sky, the Fire Eye, lanterns that light as they progress, the Cinder drifting toward where to go, and Elias's suit seams. If those are readable, the player can play.

## Why not "just waiting"?
Stillness is not idle time. It is a skill with timing and risk:
- You choose **when** and **where** to stop (behind cover, in a calm pocket).
- Enemies keep acting while you are calm.
- Hold times grow: 2.5 s, 3, 4, 3, 5 and finally 10 s under wind that tries to push you off.
- Motion is still the other half of the game: traversal, combat openings and flight need real skill.

## What makes it new
- The game **punishes** panic and **rewards** calm through the world, not through a score.
- The climax is a quiet recognition, not a boss.
- The six-line ladder is both story, progress bar and map.
